Why we built Consul: private wealth intelligence for the people who manage their own money

The analysis a private wealth team prepares for a client — available to anyone with a brokerage account and the discipline to run it themselves.

By Srivatsa Kasagar · May 14, 2026 · 6 min read
Share: LinkedIn X

If you have built real wealth and chosen to manage it yourself, you already know the strange gap you live in.

You are sophisticated enough to run your own portfolio. You read the filings, you understand the trade-offs, you have opinions about duration and concentration. But the moment you want a second set of eyes on the whole picture, your options collapse. A private bank will give you that caliber of analysis, then charge roughly one percent of everything you own for it, every year, forever. A robo-advisor will rebalance you into a model and call it advice. A spreadsheet will give you numbers without ever telling you what they mean.

We built Consul to close that gap. It is an AI Chief of Staff for your portfolio: the analysis a private wealth team would prepare for a client, available to anyone with a brokerage account and the discipline to manage it themselves.

The insight that started it

Spend time near private wealth management and you notice something uncomfortable. Most of what justifies the fee is not secret. The valuable part of a quarterly review is two things stacked together: a set of well-understood calculations, and a human who can explain what those calculations mean for you.

The calculations are deterministic. Volatility, Sharpe ratio, value at risk, beta against a benchmark, sector concentration, the twenty-year drag of an expense ratio. These are formulas. They do not require a relationship manager. They require the right inputs and the arithmetic done correctly.

The judgment is the rest. Knowing that a 0.31 sector concentration score means you are one bad quarter in one industry away from a problem. Knowing that an unrealized loss is also a tax-loss harvesting opportunity, subject to the rules of your jurisdiction. Knowing which of five flagged issues actually deserves your attention this month.

For decades, the only way to get both was to hand someone a percentage of your net worth. We did not think that was a law of nature. We thought it was a packaging problem.

How Consul works

Connect or enter your holdings and Consul builds a complete picture of your portfolio: total value across currencies, unrealized gains and losses, risk metrics, sector and asset-class weights, fee exposure, and the tax situation specific to your account type and country.

Then you can simply ask. "Explain my full risk exposure." "Which holdings are costing me the most in fees?" "How protected am I in a twenty percent correction?" "Identify tax-loss harvesting opportunities." Consul answers in plain language, grounded entirely in your actual positions, and it leads with the bottom line instead of burying it.

When you want the full picture, Consul assembles a briefing the way an advisory team would. A risk officer assesses concentration and drawdown. A tax strategist surfaces harvest candidates and account placement. A fee auditor projects the long-term cost of your expensive holdings. A diversification analyst checks your spread against your benchmark. Their findings are synthesized into one clear document with a short list of prioritized actions, ordered by what matters most.

It runs stress tests too. Rate hikes, recessions, inflation spikes, a tech correction, a credit crisis, and several more, each showing what your specific portfolio would do under that pressure. Not the market in the abstract. Your money, in that scenario.

The rule we refused to break

Here is the design decision we are proudest of, and the reason we trust Consul with our own portfolios.

The AI never does the math.

Every number you see is computed by deterministic financial code, not generated by a language model. The model's job is to read those verified figures and tell you what they mean. It is the narrator, never the calculator. When a figure is missing, Consul says so plainly rather than inventing one.

This matters more than it might sound. The well-known failure of AI is that it can state something false with complete confidence. In most applications that is annoying. In wealth management it is disqualifying. By separating the computation from the explanation, we get the part language models are genuinely excellent at, clear reasoning in plain English, without ever risking the part they are bad at, arithmetic under pressure. Every number on your screen traces back to a formula you could check by hand.

The philosophy in one line

The math is deterministic, and the model writes the story.

Who this is for

Consul is built for the self-directed investor who has outgrown simple tools but does not want to surrender a percentage of their wealth to get serious analysis. People who hold individual stocks and ETFs across multiple accounts. People who want to understand their concentration risk before the market reminds them of it. People who would rather see a stress test in advance than learn the lesson in real time.

It is not a robo-advisor and it does not trade for you. It does not push products, because it has none to push. It gives you the clearest possible understanding of what you own and what it means, and then it leaves the decisions where they belong, with you.

See what your portfolio actually means

Most people managing their own money have never seen their portfolio analyzed the way a private bank would analyze it. Not because the analysis is rare, but because it has always been locked behind a fee.

It does not have to be. Add your holdings and ask Consul your first question. In a few minutes you will know your real risk exposure, your hidden fees, your concentration, and the handful of actions actually worth taking.
